# How other agents pick their models (field survey, 2026-11-01)

Why this exists: the Flash-Lite "strong" slot and the 10-a-day cap were inherited from the first spec and no longer matched where the money goes (the Sonnet writer). Before changing them we looked at how the agents people actually use this autumn, and the open-source frameworks behind them, choose and route models. Web research done on 2026-11-01; "[reported]" marks press or community claims, "[confirmed]" vendor documentation, "[not found]" means nothing is published.

## Published measurements of "cheap executor, escalate on signal"

- **Anthropic, choosing a model and the advisor pattern** (2026-04-09 blog; platform docs): "Tuning effort is often a better lever than switching models." Executor/advisor: a cheap model runs the loop and escalates on demand. Sonnet with an Opus advisor: +2.7 points on SWE-bench Multilingual at 11.9% lower cost; Haiku with an Opus advisor 41.2% vs 19.7% on BrowseComp at 85% less than Sonnet alone. Orchestrator/workers (Haiku workers under Opus): 32% cheaper, 4 points lower, "about what a mix of low and medium effort without subagents would give." Rule stated: a multi-model setup must beat the single model's whole score-versus-spend curve. platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence · claude.com/blog/the-advisor-strategy
- **LangChain × NVIDIA NeMo Switchyard benchmark** (2026-08-11): a 30B executor, a Flash-Lite judge voting each turn, two consecutive "off track" verdicts escalate the session to a frontier model. 145 agentic tasks: 7% of turns reached the frontier model, cost $11.45 → $3.00 (−74%), accuracy 86.0 → 80.0; the judge was 21% of routed spend. Break-even rule: minimum offload = judge cost ÷ (expensive − cheap). langchain.com/blog/switchyard-agent-routing-benchmark
- **Qolca** (2026-07-15): the exact "small model for classification and tool prep, big model for the reply" pattern in a live sales assistant; no numbers published. qolca.org/blog/model-routing-small-models-big-models
- Not found: any engineering post on model routing from Sierra, Decagon, Intercom Fin, Character.AI or Replika.

## What SoberCoach takes from this

1. **Explicit per-task slots, not a learned router.** OpenClaw and Hermes both expose named slots (primary, utility, compaction, dreaming, approval) with ordered fallbacks and no classifier. SoberCoach already had this shape (tool pass, strong decision, writer, small-talk writer, dreaming). The 2026-11-01 revision names the slots in `.env.example` and keeps every fallback chain free of repeated models.
2. **The cheap model keeps the main loop; the big model is pulled in on a signal.** Anthropic's advisor data and the Switchyard numbers both say the savings come from keeping routine turns off the frontier model, and that quality costs a few points when the escalation signal is wrong. SoberCoach escalates on written signals (emotional, reflect, binge, heavy day, over goal, rapid pacing, late night, multi-entry edit), which is the same idea with code instead of a judge model, so there is no judge cost.
3. **Effort before model.** Anthropic's guidance and the Muse and dots designs (one flagship with effort levels) both point at reasoning effort as the first lever. SoberCoach now sends low effort on the Sonnet decision pass (it was unset, and a multi-entry edit cost $0.024 before, $0.018 after) and exposes `SOUL_STRONG_REASONING` for the strong writer.
4. **Budgets live outside the model.** No framework ships a dollar cap; LiteLLM and OpenRouter workspace budgets do. SoberCoach's per-user daily budget (`USER_MODEL_BUDGET_USD_PER_DAY`, default $0.15, so a 30-day heavy user stays under the $4.50 ceiling) is the same control inside the harness, with the degrade path the products use: finish the turn with a cheaper writer rather than go silent.
5. **A second strong model from another vendor was not buying anything.** Flash-Lite in the strong decision slot was chosen on τ²-Bench and a bakeoff that only measured state accuracy, which both candidates passed; the planned human rating never happened. With the writer on Sonnet, the slot handled at most 10 decision retries a day. It is now Sonnet (fallback Haiku), two models in total, measured in `docs/DECISION_RECORD.md`.

What we did not adopt: a learned router (OpenRouter auto, RouteLLM) because a 2-model, rule-driven setup is auditable and the rules are already the clinical ones; a judge model per turn (Switchyard) because at 21% of spend it would cost more than the escalations it prevents at our scale.
